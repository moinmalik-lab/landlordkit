Wear and tear is not deductible — anywhere

No state allows a landlord to charge a tenant for ordinary use. The distinction courts apply is roughly this:

Wear and tear — your cost

Faded paint, worn carpet traffic lanes, small nail holes, loose door handles, minor scuffs, grout discolouration, worn appliance finish.

Damage — deductible

Burns, pet stains through to the pad, large holes, broken fixtures, missing appliances, unapproved paint colours, removed carpet.

Depreciation is why full replacement rarely holds up

If a carpet with an eight-year life is ruined in year six, you have lost two years of its value, not eight. Charging the tenant for a brand-new carpet is the single most common reason a landlord loses a deposit case. Set the item's useful life and its age below and the calculator applies the reduction for you.

Before you send this

  • Check your state's deadline for returning a deposit. Missing it can forfeit your right to deduct anything, and several states impose penalties of two to three times the deposit.
  • Attach a receipt or written estimate for every line. An unsupported figure is the first thing challenged.
  • Compare against your move-in record. Without it, the tenant's account of the original condition usually prevails.
  • Send to the tenant's forwarding address in writing, and keep proof of posting.
